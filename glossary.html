Legal Terms Simplified

Government forms use words most people never meet anywhere else. Here is what the common ones actually mean.

Terms

6 terms

  • A document that proves you permanently reside in a particular state or region.

    Example: It is often required for state government schemes and for state quota college admissions.

    Ask the assistant about this

  • A written statement you swear is true, signed in front of an authorised officer. It is used when you have no document to prove something.

    Example: If your name is spelled differently on two documents, an affidavit explains which is correct.

    Ask the assistant about this

  • A photocopy of your own document that you sign yourself to confirm it is a true copy. No notary or gazetted officer is needed.

    Example: Photocopy your Aadhaar card, then sign across the bottom of the copy.

    Ask the assistant about this

  • The total money earned in one year by everyone in your household put together, not just you.

    Example: If you earn ₹2,00,000 and your mother earns ₹1,00,000, your annual family income is ₹3,00,000.

    Ask the assistant about this

  • The person who receives the money or benefit from a scheme.

    Example: In a scholarship, the student is the beneficiary.

    Ask the assistant about this

  • A senior government officer whose signature is accepted as official verification.

    Example: A headmaster of a government school or a tahsildar can usually sign as one.

    Ask the assistant about this
